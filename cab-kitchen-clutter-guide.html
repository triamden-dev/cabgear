Cab Kitchen & Clutter Control: 5 Gear Picks for Over-the-Road Drivers

By Clifford Cruickshank, CDL-A driver · Updated October 2026

A truck cab is your office, kitchen and bedroom in less floor space than most closets. Two things decide whether a long week out feels manageable: whether you can find what you need without digging, and whether you can eat something other than truck-stop food. This guide covers both.

I've driven commercially since 2017, OTR and regional, hauling reefer and dry van freight. I run a 2022 Kenworth T680 sleeper with a built-in fridge, and I reheat home-cooked meals in a microwave. If your truck doesn't have those, the gear below is how you get close.

How I picked these: I haven't tested these five products myself. I chose them from their Amazon listings, published specs and the number of customer reviews, and I've noted what to check before you buy. When I use one, I'll update this page with what I find.

Organization

Skip the seat-back organizer. Get a visor organizer.

Seat-back organizers are sold everywhere as trucker gear, and I think they're a waste of money. In a truck the seats are usually pushed all the way back, so getting behind one to pull something out is impractical. A visor organizer puts the same things right above you, where you can reach them.

Pick: High Road visor organizer and document holder · Alternative: WYNEX MOLLE visor organizer

The High Road model is built around paperwork and cards, with a document pocket and smaller slots, which suits logs, bills of lading and fuel cards. The WYNEX uses a MOLLE panel if you'd rather clip on pouches. Both are sold for cars and trucks, and semi visors are larger than car visors, so check the strap length against your visor before buying.

XERGUR 2-in-1 steering wheel tray

Brand: XERGUR · Fits on the steering wheel or a headrest

A flat surface for a meal or paperwork while you're parked. Check the product dimensions against your own steering wheel before you buy: semi wheels are larger and sit flatter than car wheels, and this tray is designed around passenger vehicles. Use it only when the truck is parked.

Hot meals on the road

My routine: my wife cooks a week of meals before I head out, they go in the truck's fridge, and I reheat them in the microwave. When they run out, it's a Walmart rotisserie chicken and potato salad, or Subway for a change. That keeps me off truck-stop food most of the week. No microwave in your truck? A 12V heated lunch box plugs into the cab's power socket and warms food you packed or bought at a grocery store. The two below are the most-reviewed options we found. Keeping food cold

Feelfunn 40QT dual-zone 12V fridge/freezer

Brand: Feelfunn · 40 quarts · Two temperature zones · 12/24V DC and AC power (per listing)

A compressor fridge that runs on the truck's DC power. The two zones let you freeze in one side and refrigerate in the other, so one grocery run can cover more of the week. A 12V fridge is a bigger purchase than anything else here, so read the fridge checklist before you buy any model.

Where to start

  • Lowest cost: a visor organizer.
  • Biggest daily difference: a heated lunch box.
  • Biggest investment: a 12V fridge. Measure your space and check your power setup first.
