12V Fridge Buying Checklist for Truck Drivers

By Clifford Cruickshank, CDL-A driver · Updated October 2026

Disclosure: TriAmden Cab Gear plans to join affiliate programs, including Amazon Associates.

A good 12V fridge can cut what you spend on food and give you better choices on the road. It's also a few hundred dollars and a permanent piece of your living space. Work through this list before you pick a model.

1. Space

  • Measure the spot: width, depth and height, plus room for the lid to open.
  • Leave clearance around the vents. Compressor fridges need airflow to run efficiently.
  • Plan how you'll secure it. Anything loose in a cab moves in a hard stop.
  • A door or lid that locks. Truck movement and vibration can pop a fridge open while you're driving and dump the contents across the cab. A secure latch or lock is the first thing I'd look for.

2. Cooling type

  • Compressor fridges hold a set temperature and many can freeze.
  • Thermoelectric coolers only cool a certain amount below the air around them, so they struggle in a hot cab.
  • Dual-zone models let you run a freezer and a fridge side at once.

3. Power while parked

  • Know how your truck handles power during your 10-hour break: APU, battery bank, shore power or idling.
  • Anti-idling rules vary by state and city. Don't plan on idling to run the fridge.
  • Look for low-voltage battery protection so the fridge shuts off before it drains your batteries.
  • Check whether the model runs on both 12V and 24V DC, and whether it has an AC adapter for home.

4. Living with it

  • Noise: you'll sleep next to it. Look for stated noise levels and read reviews that mention them.
  • Drain plug and removable baskets make cleaning easier.
  • App control is optional. A clear physical display matters more.
  • Warranty: check how long it lasts and whether it covers the compressor, the most expensive part to replace.

5. Your carrier

  • Some fleets limit appliances, inverters or how equipment is mounted. Ask before you buy if you drive a company truck.

Models covered on this site

We currently cover the Feelfunn 40QT dual-zone as a mid-priced option. Premium models from brands such as ICECO cost considerably more.

My own Kenworth came with a built-in fridge, so I haven't bought a portable one. If I were shopping, I'd weigh capacity, power draw, dimensions, cooling performance and reliability, and I wouldn't buy one without a locking door.
